LED lighting guide

Quick answers to common questions about choosing LED lights.

Which LED bulb is best for home use?

Choose by brightness, colour and the room. Brightness is measured in lumens: a 9–10 W LED bulb gives roughly 800–900 lumens, about the same as an old 60 W bulb. Warm white (2700–3000 K) feels cosy in bedrooms and living rooms, while cool daylight (5700–6500 K) suits kitchens, study tables and shops. Also check the pack for the BIS registration mark, which carries an R-number.

How many watts of LED light do I need for a room?

Think in lumens rather than watts. As a rough guide, plan about 10–20 lumens per square foot for general room lighting, and more for kitchens and work areas. A 10 × 12 ft bedroom (120 sq ft) is comfortable with roughly 1,200–2,400 lumens, for example one 15–18 W LED panel or two 12 W LED bulbs.

Warm white, neutral white or cool white: which should I choose?

Colour temperature is measured in kelvin (K). Warm white (about 2700–3000 K) gives a soft yellowish light for bedrooms, living rooms and pooja rooms. Neutral white (about 4000 K) looks natural and works almost anywhere. Cool white or daylight (about 5700–6500 K) is crisp and bright, good for kitchens, offices and shops.

How much electricity do LED lights save?

LED bulbs use roughly 80–90% less electricity than old filament bulbs for the same light, and about a third less than CFLs. An 18–20 W LED tube light gives similar light to a 36–40 W fluorescent tube light, at about half the power.

How long do LED lights last?

Good-quality LED bulbs and tube lights are commonly rated for about 15,000–25,000 hours. At 8 hours a day, 15,000 hours is roughly five years. Heat, fully enclosed fittings and frequent voltage fluctuations can shorten their life.

Do LED lights work with voltage fluctuations and home inverters?

Many LED bulbs and tube lights are designed to work over a wide voltage range and run on home inverters. Check the voltage range printed on the pack, and use surge protection or a stabiliser if your area has frequent fluctuations.

Which lights should I use in bathrooms and outdoors?

Check the IP rating on the light. IP44 or higher handles splashes in bathrooms, and IP65 or higher is recommended for outdoor lights exposed to rain and dust.

Can LED lights be dimmed?

Only if the bulb or fitting is marked dimmable and you use a dimmer made for LEDs. Non-dimmable LEDs on a dimmer may flicker, buzz or fail early.

What is CRI and why does it matter?

CRI (colour rendering index) shows how naturally a light displays colours, on a scale up to 100. CRI 80 or above is fine for most homes. CRI 90 or above is better where colours matter, such as clothing and textile showrooms, dressing areas and artwork.

What is the difference between panel lights, downlights and tube lights?

Panel lights are slim square or round fittings, often set into false ceilings, that spread light evenly over a room. Downlights are smaller recessed lights that throw light downwards, used in rows or to highlight an area. Tube lights and battens are long fittings mounted on walls or ceilings, common in homes, shops and offices.

What size chandelier or hanging light suits my room?

A common rule of thumb: add the room's length and width in feet, and use that number in inches as the chandelier's diameter. A 12 × 14 ft room suits a chandelier about 26 inches wide. Over a dining table, hang it so the bottom is about 30–36 inches above the tabletop.

What is smart lighting?

Smart lighting lets you switch lights on and off, dim them or change their colour from a phone app, voice assistant, remote or timer. Smart bulbs fit standard holders, so many setups need no rewiring. Ask us which smart lighting options suit your home or project.
